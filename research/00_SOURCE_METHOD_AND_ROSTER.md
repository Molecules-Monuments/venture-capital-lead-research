# Source Method, Outcome Labels, and Research Roster

Access date for web sources: 2026-07-20.

## What “proven” means here

This project does not infer skill from fame, AUM, private valuations, portfolio
logos, or a single winner. Venture outcomes are power-law distributed, public
fund-return data are incomplete, and published frameworks also market the fund
to founders and LPs. Practitioner eligibility and causal support are therefore
separate questions.

Use these labels:

1. **Fund-return verified** — the exact fund/vintage has LP or audited cash-flow
   evidence with net/gross status, DPI, TVPI, IRR, valuation date, and realized
   split. CalPERS explains why interim IRR alone is insufficient and why its own
   active-fund data lag; its disclosures are useful but not universal truth
   ([CalPERS private-equity fund performance](https://www.calpers.ca.gov/investments/about-investment-office/investment-organization/pep-fund-performance)).
2. **Portfolio-outcome verified** — an issuer filing or transaction record
   confirms ownership/involvement and a public or acquired outcome. This does
   not prove cost basis, proceeds, carry, or net fund return. For example,
   Sequoia’s YouTube financing and Google’s $1.65 billion acquisition are
   independently documented ([YouTube financing](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-receives-35m-in-funding-from/),
   [Google closing announcement](https://googlepress.blogspot.com/2006/11/google-closes-acquisition-of-youtube_13.html));
   an SEC filing documents Sequoia’s Airbnb ownership
   ([Airbnb Schedule 13G/A](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1559720/000119312524251309/d829566dsc13ga.htm)).
3. **Practice exemplar** — a source exposes a method worth testing, but the
   method’s causal contribution to returns is not established.

No file in Version 3.0 calls a fund or person financially proven unless the
specific claim passes the first or second label. A successful company makes a
practitioner worth studying; it does not validate every statement they make.

## Claim-level evidence grading

Score 0–3 on each dimension: claim–source fit, independence/conflicts,
denominator completeness, outcome validity, method reproducibility,
role/stage/sector/geography transfer, and temporal alignment.

- **A (18–21):** direct, independently checkable, no fatal limitation.
- **B (14–17):** strong with one material limitation.
- **C (10–13):** useful local/context evidence; corroboration required.
- **D (6–9):** incentive-laden anecdote or incomplete method.
- **E (0–5):** discovery lead only.

A selected-winner story, circular citation, reconstructed post-success memo,
self-reported return without basis, wrong-stage analogy, or search snippet caps
the claim at C. A fund blog can be A evidence that the fund publicly espouses a
practice and D evidence that the practice caused performance.

Normally, a system rule needs one A/B mechanism source, one independent
corroboration, explicit counterevidence or a documented failed search, and a
precommitted eval capable of falsifying the change. External evidence never
sets a scoring weight; weights require local labeled data and chronological
backtesting.

## Adversarial source protocol

- Reconstruct cases at decision time `T`; later facts are outcomes, not inputs.
- Track all sourced, screened, diligenced, invested, rejected, written-off,
  realized, and active cases when a denominator is available.
- Distinguish firm, fund, vintage, partner, sourcing, selection, board work,
  company outcome, gross mark, and realized net return.
- Record `source_origin_id` so five articles repeating one interview remain one
  origin.
- Treat regulator complaints as allegations and decisions/settlements by their
  actual legal status.
- Ask what founder/LP behavior a public framework is designed to attract.
- Record stage, sector, geography, ownership model, and data-availability
  conditions before importing a method.
- Search for false negatives. Bessemer’s anti-portfolio is useful institutional
  dissent, but is still a selected humorous narrative rather than a complete
  rejection ledger ([Bessemer Anti-Portfolio](https://www.bvp.com/anti-portfolio)).
- Use the SEC’s cautions on selective, extracted, gross, hypothetical, and
  misleading performance marketing as a disclosure lens
  ([SEC marketing-rule guide](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/small-business-compliance-guides/investment-adviser-marketing)).

The Kauffman Foundation’s nearly 100-fund history is the central antidote to
brand halo: most funds in its sample did not beat public markets after fees,
and large branded funds were not a safe shortcut
([Kauffman report](https://www.kauffman.org/reports/we-have-met-the-enemy-and-he-is-us/)).
This historical sample is not a current ranking, but it makes outcome opacity
and denominator discipline non-optional.

Calacanis and other media-driven investors receive a stricter self-promotion
discount; Meeker-style trend reports are strong source compilations but not
proof of prediction; Thiel-style contrarian questions are hypothesis generators,
not exclusion rules. Practitioner promotion to “proven investor” requires
verified net fund evidence or at least two independently verified exceptional
portfolio outcomes across vintages with clear personal attribution. It still
does not prove personal net return without cost and realization data.

independently documented operating outcome—not social reach or a current
private mark. A founder’s advice about their company is not automatically an
investor-selection rule.

## Academic and standards spine

- Gompers, Gornall, Kaplan, and Strebulaev survey 885 institutional VCs across
  sourcing, selection, value-add, exits, and organization. This is unusually
  broad descriptive evidence, but it reports beliefs/practices, not causal
  return drivers ([NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w22587)).
- Camuffo et al.’s startup RCT and larger replication support explicit
  hypotheses and tests; transfer to investors and later-stage companies is not
  established ([original](https://iris.unibocconi.it/handle/11565/4013977),
  [replication](https://sms.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/smj.3580)).
- Structured-interview meta-analysis supports consistent questions and scoring,
  but founder work is not employee job performance and halo-free validity must
  be tested locally ([Schmidt & Rader](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdf/10.1111/j.1744-6570.1999.tb00169.x)).
- Kauffman’s historical fund analysis, Cochrane’s selection-bias work, and
  anti-portfolios prevent outcome narratives from becoming weights.
- The W3C provenance model supports entity/activity/agent lineage
  ([PROV-O](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-o/)); PostgreSQL documents indexed trigram
  similarity but not identity truth
  ([pg_trgm](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/17/pgtrgm.html)).
- OWASP, CISA, and NIST support treating files and remote text as untrusted,
  validating type/size, disabling active content, and isolating prompt injection
  ([OWASP upload](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/File_Upload_Cheat_Sheet.html),
  [CISA ransomware guide](https://www.cisa.gov/stopransomware/ransomware-guide),
  [NIST GAI profile](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf)).

Academic work generates hypotheses, denominators, and fixtures. It is not
privileged over practitioner evidence merely because it is academic, and its
weights/effects are never copied into the fund’s rubric without local data.

## Translation rule

Research is retained only if it changes an input requirement, evidence/search
method, decision decomposition, stop rule, output field, handoff, evaluation,
or customization instruction. Biography and memorable quotes without a
falsifiable operational consequence remain in the source ledger, not the
agent contract.
